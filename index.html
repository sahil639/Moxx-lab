• 01 Terminal Type

    Portrait terminal text that types itself in, a scramble of glyphs decoding just ahead of the write head, over scanlines and flicker. Drop in any font file to see it set in that face.
  • 02 Dither Lab

    Drop an image or a video and it is dithered live on the GPU — Bayer, noise or halftone, down to a few tones and a three-colour palette, with a lens that sharpens wherever the cursor rests.
  • 03 Line Relief

    A field of parallel lines pushed sideways by an image's brightness, so the picture exists only as deformation — bunching into dense bands where it steepens, thinning into dots where it spreads.
  • 04 Glyph Matrix

    A grid of small marks that reads a picture or a video and redraws it in its own alphabet — rings, bars, crosses, characters — one glyph and colour per tonal band, with a wave running through the lattice.
  • 05 Prism Core

    A soft, rounded block that glows from the inside — orbs, translucent layers and drifting motes moving through it. Turn it by hand, change its shape, view and arrangement, and build the gradient from as many colours as you like.
  • 06 Compass Mark

    A logo drawn with nothing but circles — lobes around a ring, fillets rolled into the notches, thin spiral channels cut through — with the construction lines left showing. Save versions, check it at icon sizes, export PNG or SVG.
  • 07 Brand Engine

    A seeded brand system: one number and one fixed palette make every visual for a brand — diagrams, avatars, layouts, sub-brand logos — and the same seed always gives the same picture. Riso print modes, PNG, SVG and video export.
  • 08 Jelly Pitch

    A tennis ball, a football and a basketball made of wobbly, translucent jelly on turf that bends under them. Pull them about, cut them with a knife, and print your own face into the jelly. Needs WebGPU.
  • 09 Pretext Play

    A playground for the pretext library: paragraphs measured and wrapped in plain JavaScript, with no DOM. Drag a column's width, shrink chat bubbles to their text, pour words around a moving circle, and see how the text is cut into pieces.
  • 10 Tape Deck

    A drum of cassettes, one per project, turning under a hot light. Hover a tape and a tracker reads out its name, its numbers and a few panels of noise; click and it opens. Saves itself as one offline file.
